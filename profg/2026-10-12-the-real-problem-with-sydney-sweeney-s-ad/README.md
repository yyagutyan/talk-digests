# The Real Problem With Sydney Sweeney’s Ad

*Ed Elson argues that sports betting, and the prediction markets growing out of it, is the real harm behind the Sydney Sweeney ad debate, and lays out four rules he wants for gambling.*

Source: [The Real Problem With Sydney Sweeney’s Ad](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kss_qJoph-8) · Ed Elson · Prof G Markets · 9 min

## TL;DR

The outrage over Sydney Sweeney's ad targets the wrong thing, according to Ed Elson, because the ad sells sports betting, which he calls a drug. Since states legalized sports betting after the 2018 Supreme Court ruling, personal bankruptcies have risen 25–30% within four years, helpline calls have risen 75%, and gambling-disorder diagnoses have risen 61%. Young men are the core market, and prediction markets are the next step, since their sports contracts are legal in every state under CFTC futures rules. Elson wants gambling regulated like alcohol, with warning labels, monitored advertising, licensed distributors, and tighter rules for prediction-market sports contracts.

- About 2.5 million American adults meet the criteria for a severe gambling problem, and the National Council on Problem Gambling puts the annual social cost at $14 billion.

## Sports Betting Is the Real Harm

[▶ 00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kss_qJoph-8&t=0s)

Elson argues the outrage over Sydney Sweeney's ad targets the wrong thing. The real harm is what the ad sells: sports betting, which he calls a drug.

- About 2.5 million American adults meet the criteria for a severe gambling problem. The National Council on Problem Gambling puts the annual social cost at $14 billion.
- After states legalized sports betting following the 2018 Supreme Court ruling, personal bankruptcies rose 25–30% within four years, helpline calls rose 75%, and gambling-disorder diagnoses rose 61%.
- Young men are the core market. Elson links online betting to male loneliness, and says more than half of young men own an online sportsbook account.

Prediction markets are the next step. Because their sports contracts are classified as futures under the CFTC, they are legal in every state, unlike DraftKings bets. Elson predicts the Supreme Court's coming ruling will change these rules.

He wants gambling treated like alcohol:

1. Warning labels on every bet
2. Tightly monitored advertising
3. Licensed distributors and ID checks
4. Prediction-market sports contracts regulated like sports bets, while economic contracts stay regulated as futures

Elson says the line to draw is between betting on the Fed funds rate and betting on the Knicks.
