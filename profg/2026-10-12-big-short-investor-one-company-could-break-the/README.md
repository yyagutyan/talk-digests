# “Big Short” Investor: One Company Could Break The AI Boom

*Steve Eisman lays out why he sees concentration risk, high Treasury yields and off-balance-sheet AI debt as threats to the AI trade, and which signals he is watching.*

Source: [“Big Short” Investor: One Company Could Break The AI Boom](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc) · Ed Elson and Steve Eisman · Prof G Markets · 50 min

## TL;DR

Steve Eisman argues that AI labs are nervous, losing market share and have no moat, and that the AI chain depends heavily on OpenAI and Anthropic. He says high Treasury yields, AI debt that may sit off balance sheet, and a rating-sensitive Oracle could strain the buildout, and that the AI narrative drives the market. For investors, he points to Anthropic's 2026 results and S-1, open-weight share gains and the 10-year yield as the signals to watch.

## Key takeaways

- Eisman says AI model businesses have no moat, because model leads change daily, unlike Google's search moat, which held for decades.
- He says token maxing is over: firms that pushed staff to use AI nonstop blew through budgets within months, and open-weight models took large market share by summer.
- About 70% of hyperscalers' AI revenue comes from OpenAI and Anthropic, so he sees the whole AI chain as exposed to those two companies succeeding.
- For Anthropic, he says the 2025 numbers matter little; the useful comparison is first-half versus second-half 2026 results, plus the adjusted operating profitability figures.
- He calls a 5% 10-year Treasury yield the market's Rubicon and sees a correction as near-certain unless rates fall back below 5%.
- He suspects AI companies move debt off balance sheet to protect credit ratings, a practice he says is not yet quantified and that auditors may accept.
- He is most concerned about Oracle, rated triple-B-minus, because a downgrade to junk would be ugly, and about CoreWeave, while he doesn't lose sleep over Meta, Google or Microsoft.
- He says his FICO short is independent of AI, since VantageScore could reach 50% share and the monopoly is ending, and his advice for oil and rates is to hang tight and reduce some risk if you want to.

## Doom talk as a moat play, and why token maxing is over

[▶ 00:01:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=90s)

Eisman calls AI doom talk a business play. He sees "absolutely no evidence" that AI is near AGI: models are next-word retrieval systems that don't think. He doubts senior executives believe the world will end.

What changed, in his view, is that **token maxing** is over. Firms that pushed staff to use AI nonstop blew through their budgets within months. By summer, enterprises were cost-conscious and open-weight models were taking large market share.

His read: incumbents without a moat want a manufactured crisis that brings regulation they can shape into a duopoly.

Reuters reported Anthropic's 2025 revenue at $4.6 billion, up more than 1,000%, with an operating loss above $8 billion. The nearly $42 billion net loss is mostly a non-cash charge, so Eisman focuses on the $8 billion. He says the 2025 numbers don't matter.

His theory: Anthropic is listing now because the first half of 2026 looks strong. He expects slowdowns in Q3 and Q4, so he'd compare H1 with H2 2026.

## Watch 2026 Results, Not Old Numbers

[▶ 00:08:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=486s)

Eisman says the Reuters reporting on 2024 and 2025 results will matter little to most people. Attention will be on 2026, especially how the company's comments on the second half compare with the first half. He also points to reported quarterly "adjusted operating profitability," which he says raises big questions about what the adjusted figure actually means.

## Nervous, losing share, and no moat

[▶ 00:09:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=545s)

Eisman doesn't know whether the AI companies are truly in trouble, but he thinks they're nervous. He sees them losing market share and signs of a price war breaking out, which he calls very bad. He repeats his CNBC point that the business has no moat. Google's search moat held for decades, but model leads now change daily, so nothing protects what these companies do.

## Concentration risk: the AI chain runs through two labs

[▶ 00:09:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=599s)

Eisman's main worry is **concentration risk**. Nvidia's revenue grew over 100%, yet 70% of its accounts receivable came from five accounts. For the hyperscalers, 70% of AI revenue comes from OpenAI and Anthropic.

Eisman says the whole chain flows to those two companies. If they succeed, AI will look like a triumph a year from now. If they run into trouble, the whole chain is in trouble.

## Whether investors have priced in AI risk

[▶ 00:10:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=659s)

The host asks whether investors understand circular financing and concentration risk in AI, noting Nvidia looks cheap on forward earnings. Eisman says he has no idea what the market has priced in and never knows. He sees a positive AI narrative and a negative one, and thinks it's unclear which will win. He expects a better answer within about six months.

## Hidden Risk in 2007 Versus Open Risk in AI

[▶ 00:12:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=738s)

Eisman recalls that a Bear Stearns fund holding subprime paper blew up in May 2007, and that equity analysts missed it because it was a fixed-income story. Understanding it meant immersing yourself in fixed income. Today's AI risk is a tech story that most people grasp. Eisman sees massive concentration risk, which depends on OpenAI and Anthropic succeeding. He doesn't know whether they will.

## Off-balance-sheet AI debt hides risk from ratings

[▶ 00:13:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=839s)

Eisman can't yet say how much of the roughly $500 billion of AI debt raised this year is off balance sheet, but says it is not insignificant. He thinks firms hide it to protect credit ratings, since agencies don't count such debt.

Meta's near-$30 billion Louisiana data center deal is his example. Its 10-K has paragraphs of auditor hedging. Eisman reads them as auditors uneasy but deferring to Meta, as in Enron.

## The off-balance-sheet share is still unknown

[▶ 00:16:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1004s)

Eisman's main caveat is that he doesn't yet know how much of the roughly $500 billion in debt sits off balance sheet. He is still working out how the total splits between on-balance-sheet and off-balance-sheet debt, so he can't say how large the issue is. His caveat applies to the auditors' view that the debt is fine.

## Protecting credit ratings through off-balance-sheet debt

[▶ 00:17:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1058s)

Eisman's view is that a larger debt figure would make these companies very nervous about their credit ratings. He expects them to do everything they can to preserve those ratings, including moving debt into off-balance-sheet vehicles that arguably should stay on the balance sheet, as long as their auditors agree that the treatment is acceptable.

## Why a 5% 10-year yield could trigger a correction

[▶ 00:20:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1230s)

Eisman calls the 5% yield on the 10-year the market's "Rubicon." Nobody knows in advance, but unless rates fall back below 5%, he sees a correction as near-certain.

Higher rates squeeze borrowers:

- **Housing:** a 6% mortgage becomes 7%+, so fewer people buy.
- **Corporate and AI debt:** CoreWeave raised money at 9% six months ago; he estimates it would now cost about 11%.
- **Deficits:** they grow larger.

Eisman says the economy gets creaky as rates rise, which is what makes everyone nervous.

## Rising rates squeeze AI data center debt

[▶ 00:22:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1337s)

Eisman agrees higher rates could strain AI debt. He treats data centers as real estate: builders expect returns many times their cost, and rates 100 basis points higher make the deal harder. Companies would have to charge customers more, and he doesn't know whether customers will pay. The interviewer adds that CoreWeave, with far less money than hyperscalers, may struggle.

## Oracle's Force Majeure Notice Is About Its Credit Rating

[▶ 00:23:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1429s)

Oracle sent Blue Owl a force majeure notice over Project Jupiter, a data center campus in New Mexico. The interviewer read it as Oracle saying it is not liable if something goes wrong. Eisman sees it differently: Oracle is worried about its credit rating. Oracle is rated triple B minus, one level above junk, and he expects it to do everything it can to avoid a downgrade to junk.

## Oracle: Weak, but Not Clearly the Weakest

[▶ 00:24:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1475s)

Asked whether Oracle has the weakest balance sheet in the AI trade, Eisman says it is weak but not clearly the weakest among the larger companies. He would not put it below a peer. His concern is the rating: at a **triple-B-minus** rating, he says, things can get ugly.

## Which AI Companies Carry the Real Risk

[▶ 00:24:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1499s)

Eisman says he doesn't lose sleep over Meta, Google or Microsoft. His concern is Oracle, CoreWeave and the other companies building out AI.

He also warns that if something bad happened to Anthropic or OpenAI, there would be nowhere to hide, because they are too important to the entire AI sector.

## Fed insurance, but the 10-year is the real worry

[▶ 00:25:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1541s)

Eisman reads the Fed's first rate hike, with inflation last at 3.4%, as insurance and expects another may follow. He is more worried about the 10-year Treasury yield. Higher oil prices and nervousness explain part of it, but he argues AI's $500 billion of issuance this year is crowding out Treasuries. Without AI, he says, the 10-year would be much lower.

## AI debt crowding out Treasuries

[▶ 00:26:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1610s)

Eisman argues that long-term AI borrowing competes with the government for the same money. Microsoft and Google fund data centers with roughly 10-year debt. On top of a $6 trillion Treasury deficit this year, he adds about $500 billion of AI debt, a **crowding-out effect** that he links to the high 10-year yield. He says the Treasury's attempt to buy long-term bonds by issuing short-term debt, known as operation twist, has failed. Since then, he says, the 10-year yield has risen from 4.75% to 5.21%, and the Treasury has lost credibility.

## Why Eisman Rejects the Yield-Mispricing Argument

[▶ 00:28:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1691s)

Eisman rejects the claim that Treasury yields are wrong, calling the market very efficient. What would it be wrong about, he asks, given the war, higher oil prices and record diesel costs? If inflation falls, yields fall too. He doesn't get the argument.

The AI bubble story, he adds, is about a year old: long for markets, short in history.

## Dot-Com Lessons Explain the Resilience; Concentration Is the Worry

[▶ 00:29:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1792s)

Eisman says the market's resilience hasn't surprised him. Investors learned from the dot-com era that the new thing usually wins, so they now treat AI leaders as the next Amazon of 2001. He has invested in these companies himself and cut some risk. His main worry is extreme concentration; it may ease in a year or two, or may not.

## Hedging AI holdings by shorting against the box

[▶ 00:31:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1915s)

Asked how he has cut risk over the past year, Steve Eisman says that in the past month he shorted part of his AI-related holdings against the box. He keeps the long shares and sells short against them, which is the hedge the interviewer asks him about later.

## Not a recommendation, but a January deadline

[▶ 00:32:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1935s)

Asked whether he would recommend his move as a hedging strategy, Eisman says he is not recommending it to anyone. He told viewers it is an option, but it must be unwound by the end of January. Otherwise, he says, the IRS will treat it as a different outcome. The transcript cuts off before he says how, so the tax treatment is unknown here.

## Narrative drives the market, not valuation

[▶ 00:32:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=1955s)

Asked about a Shiller CAPE near 41, the second-highest reading ever, Eisman says valuation isn't his focus. The AI narrative drives prices: if it holds, stocks rise; if it breaks, they correct sharply whatever the valuation. The better question, he argues, is whether the fundamentals and the narrative will change, not whether the market is too expensive to buy.

## Tracking open-weight share and Anthropic's S-1

[▶ 00:33:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2035s)

Eisman's main check on the AI narrative is how much share open-weight models are taking from more expensive models. He is also eager to read Anthropic's S-1 filing to understand what is going on at the company.

## A resilient market, with oil and rates in control

[▶ 00:36:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2185s)

Eisman's main takeaway is that the market has been unusually resilient despite a war, higher inflation and still-strong US growth, and should be respected. He won't predict the fourth quarter. For now, oil prices and interest rates matter more than the AI narrative. If the war ends, he expects a return to a more normal narrative.

## Oil and rates: hang tight

[▶ 00:37:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2249s)

Asked whether investors can act on oil prices and interest rates, Eisman says the best move is to do nothing. Investors are suddenly expected to be geopolitical analysts, but nobody can predict how the war will go. A position that wins if oil rises loses if the war ends tomorrow, and the reverse holds too. His advice: hang tight, and reduce some risk if you want to.

## Mostly long, with less risk and no call on AI collapse

[▶ 00:38:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2310s)

Eisman says he is mostly long, holds some shorts, and has reduced risk because, like everyone else, he is nervous about the AI narrative. He won't make a major call that the whole AI story will implode. "I'm just not willing to do that." He thinks that call is premature, though it may turn out to be right.

## FICO's Monopoly Is Ending

[▶ 00:39:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2352s)

Eisman is short FICO, which has long held a near-monopoly on US mortgage credit scores. He says it raised prices 1,600% in five years, angering its regulator. The regulator helped create rival VantageScore, about 8% share, which could reach 50% within months. Rocket Mortgage is switching to it, and FICO fell nearly 20%. His thesis: the monopoly is ending.

## Spotting a short: FICO as a non-AI bet

[▶ 00:41:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2488s)

Eisman says his FICO short is independent of AI and the economy: it "lives in its own universe." An X post attacking FICO first caught his attention. He has seen that when a financial-services company really angers its regulator, the result is rarely good. Over time it became clear the critic wanted an alternative to FICO and would make sure one existed.

## Sleep as a position-sizing test

[▶ 00:42:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2550s)

Eisman says sleep is a major factor in his positioning: "When I don't sleep I start to go crazy." He says investors need to be comfortable with their positions and can't live in a panic. If that means selling, cutting risk or shorting to feel calmer, he argues that is the right move, and he thinks this holds for everyone.

## Fear that the government did not know

[▶ 00:43:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2603s)

Eisman says sleep was never the problem. He believed the planet was going to burn, and people who came to his office left in tears. He and his partners assumed that "Surely the government knows what we know," and that it would act. It did not. Eisman says officials were completely ignorant, which is why the crisis got as bad as it did.

## Watch Anthropic and OpenAI

[▶ 00:44:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2660s)

Eisman says the signal to watch is Anthropic and OpenAI. Within the next year, trouble at either would hit hard because the business is not yet diversified; he expects that to change in five years. He cites roughly 70% of AI hyperscaler revenue coming purely from these two companies, which he calls incredible concentration risk. If one of them gets into trouble, he says, everybody is in trouble.

## Recession risk versus systemic risk

[▶ 00:45:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2704s)

Eisman hopes the government won't make the AI labs too big to fail: "I really don't want to go down this road again." He says if OpenAI or Anthropic went bankrupt tomorrow, the US economy would likely fall into or near recession quickly, but that is not systemic risk and would recover in a year or two. A failure of a bank like JP Morgan, or depositors being unable to withdraw their money, would be systemic: "that's the end of the world."

## Zero Appetite for a $2 Trillion Debut

[▶ 00:46:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2769s)

Eisman has zero interest in buying into Anthropic's expected roughly $2 trillion IPO, as with SpaceX. He found it funny that SpaceX's S-1 mentioned asteroid mining, a theme of the Apple show *For All Mankind*, which he recommends. Grok, Musk's AI company inside SpaceX, takes its name from a Heinlein novel where it means deep understanding.

## Watch the AI narrative, because the market is one trade

[▶ 00:47:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PJrq_bw7bPc&t=2879s)

Eisman's advice is to keep watching the AI narrative to see which way it goes. Outside interest rates and oil, he says the market is basically one trade: only staples and healthcare, about 14% of the S&P combined, are not AI-ish, leaving roughly 86% exposed to AI to some degree. "That's the only narrative that matters."
