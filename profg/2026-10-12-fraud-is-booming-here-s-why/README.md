# Fraud Is Booming — Here’s Why

*How fraud is spreading across sports, finance and government, why the speaker blames weak enforcement, and what he proposes instead.*

Source: [Fraud Is Booming — Here’s Why](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdSTmXokY0s) · Ed Elson · Prof G Markets · 10 min

## TL;DR

Fraud is rising across sports, Wall Street and tech, with Manchester City found guilty on 114 counts and the Clippers' owner accused of faking sponsorship deals. The speaker blames weaker U.S. enforcement, citing 17 inspectors general fired, the Justice Department's anti-corruption unit cut from 36 lawyers to two, and SEC enforcement actions down more than 90% this year. The main harm he identifies is distrust: when people think the game is rigged, they stop participating. His proposed fix is enforcement beyond fines, such as license suspension, prison and reputational damage, so that the downside of fraud outweighs the upside.

## Key takeaways

- Manchester City was found guilty on 114 counts of misrepresenting about £900 million in financials since 2009 using sham contracts.
- The Clippers' owner faked sponsorship deals to pay players above the league's limit.
- First Brands and Tricolor collapsed on Wall Street, and investors filed 118 securities fraud suits in the first half of 2026.
- The speaker says SEC enforcement actions fell more than 90% this year and that 17 inspectors general were fired.
- The speaker says the Justice Department's anti-corruption unit was cut from 36 lawyers to two.
- Gallup found 89% of Americans say government corruption is widespread.
- The speaker argues the main harm of fraud is distrust, which makes people stop participating and is linked to young people's financial nihilism.
- The speaker's fix is to enforce the law beyond fines, using license suspension, prison and reputational damage, and for Manchester City he wants titles stripped, a significant fine and possible relegation.

## Fraud is spreading, and enforcement is weak

[▶ 00:00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdSTmXokY0s&t=0s)

Ed Harrison argues fraud is booming across sports, finance and tech. Manchester City was found guilty on 114 counts of misrepresenting about £900 million in financials since 2009 using sham contracts. The Clippers' owner faked sponsorship deals to pay players above the league's limit. On Wall Street, First Brands and Tricolor collapsed, and investors filed 118 securities fraud suits in the first half of 2026.

Harrison blames weaker enforcement. He says President Trump fired 17 inspectors general, cut the Justice Department's anti-corruption unit from 36 lawyers to two, and that SEC enforcement actions fell more than 90% this year. Gallup found 89% of Americans say government corruption is widespread.

The main harm, he argues, is distrust. When people think the game is rigged, they stop participating, which he links to young people's financial nihilism. Citing Charlie Munger, he says "Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome."

His fix is to enforce the law beyond fines, using license suspension, prison and reputational damage, so the downside of fraud outweighs the upside. For Manchester City, he wants titles stripped, a significant fine and possible relegation.
